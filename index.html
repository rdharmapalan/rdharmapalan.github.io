Research

Calibration: another nonchalant physics term for something subtle and fundamental. It's really an attempt to answer the question, how do we know what we know? Well, we compare it to something else we know. And how do we know that?...

As an experimental particle physicist working on neutrinos, I've spent the last few years chasing that regress.

Currently, I am analysing data collected by the ProtoDUNE experiment at CERN and developing tools for the eventual DUNE experiment.

Before coming to Hawaii, I was a research associate at the HEP Division at Argonne National Laboratory, working on detector R&D and neutrino physics. I was part of the Instrumentation group developing the next generation of ultra-fast, high-resolution photodetectors. These microchannel-plate-based PMTs have picosecond timing resolution and sub-millimeter position resolution. Our group made working prototypes as an offshoot of the LAPPD project.

I also worked with the neutrino group on the NOvA experiment on the NuMI beamline at Fermilab. My primary interest was the study of off-axis, mostly kaon-produced neutrinos from the BNB beam at the NOvA near detector.

For my Ph.D. I worked on the MiniBooNE experiment, where I measured the antineutrino neutral-current elastic cross section. Read about it in Fermilab News.

Towards the end of my graduate research, my colleagues, theorists, and I proposed a search for low-mass dark matter at a neutrino experiment with the MiniBooNE detector. We collected data for 9 months and published our first results in Phys. Rev. Letters. Here is a brief article I wrote for Fermilab News.
